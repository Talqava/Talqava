# Automations

Automations let work happen by itself at set times: a morning news digest every weekday, a check of last week's orders every Monday, a month-end reminder, or a one-off job on a certain date. You write the instructions once, choose when they run and who does them, and each run ends with a short report of what was done.

## What you'll see

Open **Automations** (the clock on the strip at the left).

- **Tabs across the top:** **All**, **Active**, **Paused**, **Run history** and **Ready-made**, each with a count.
- **One table** of everything that runs by itself, whether you wrote it or switched on a ready-made one. Each row shows its name and who does it, when it runs in plain words (for example "Every weekday at 8:00 am"), when it runs next (or **paused**), when it last ran (with **Did not work**, **Left out** or **Needs you** beside it when the last run was not a plain success), and about what it costs a month.
- **A switch on each row** turns it on or pauses it.
- **A monthly limit for one automation.** **Set a monthly limit…** under ⋯ sets the most it may spend in a calendar month. Once its runs have cost that much, its timed runs are left out until the month turns or you raise the limit, and your Inbox says so. The Cost column then shows what it has spent of its limit. Nothing is spent between runs: an automation uses your AI model only when it runs.
- **The ⋯ button on each row** opens **Run now** (or **Send one now**), **Edit**, **Open the last run** and **Delete** (or **Switch off for good**).
- **+ New automation** opens a form with **Name**, **When**, **Who does it** and **Instructions**.
- **Run history** lists every run, newest first: which automation, when, how it turned out (**Done**, **Working**, **Needs you**, **Did not work** or **Left out**, with the reason) and what it cost. The last 300 runs are kept.
- **One run at a time.** If the run before is still being worked on, or is waiting for your answer, the next one on its timer is left out and the history says so. **Run now** always runs.
- **A run that did not work, or was left out, is said in your Inbox**, so nothing fails without your knowing.
- **What it costs** is shown before you switch a ready-made one on: about how much each time and a month. It is an estimate from your AI model's list price until the automation has run; after that it is the average of its own runs.
- **Ready-made** holds the briefings described below.

## Briefings

A briefing is an automation that is already written: a report an agent sends to your Inbox. **Start a briefing**, at the top of this page, lists 50 of them in seven groups. Click a group's name to open it.

- **Your own work**: Monday plan, Friday wrap-up, End-of-day wrap, Weekly goals check and Agenda for each project are free, built from your tasks and goals. Weekly roundup of your chats and Meeting notes to actions use your AI model.
- **The outside world**: Morning news, Competitor watch, Rules and deadlines, Supplier price watch, Review replies, Blog post idea, Learning digest, Mentions watch, New video digest, Search ranking check, Idea log and Trending projects digest need only web search. **Website down alert** and **Page change watch** are free: Talqava looks at the address itself, with no AI model.
- **Money**: Overdue invoices, Bills due this week, Weekly cash check, Subscription watch and Month-end pack read Xero. Yesterday's sales reads Square. Receipts to a list reads Gmail or Outlook.
- **Customers**: Inbox triage (Gmail or Outlook), Unanswered customers (Zendesk), New leads (HubSpot, Pipedrive or Typeform), Follow-up reminders (HubSpot or Pipedrive), Quiet customers (Square), New signup prospecting and Proposal first draft (HubSpot or Pipedrive), After-hours holding replies (Gmail, Outlook, Zendesk, Freshdesk or Telegram).
- **Marketing**: Newsletter draft and Social posts for the week (from what Talqava knows of your business), Campaign results (Mailchimp).
- **Running the business**: Meeting prep, Tomorrow's day and Travel check (Google Calendar), Stock running low (Square), Open issues digest (GitLab, Jira, ClickUp, Asana, Trello or Todoist), Merge requests waiting (GitLab), Weekly review and plan (Google Calendar), Files tidy-up (free).
- **People and renewals** (free): Renewals coming up and Birthdays and anniversaries work from a list you type; Policy review reminder looks at your Workspace pages.

**Keep an eye on.** The first one under **Your own work** is your own list. Type the things you want watched, one on each line, in your own words: "Has the flour delivery been confirmed for Friday?", "Is our website up?", "Did Corner Café pay invoice 1042 yet?". An agent checks the list three times each weekday with what it can reach (the web, your connected services, your files and tasks) and writes to your Inbox only about the lines that need you now. When nothing does, it stays silent. It reads and reports; it changes nothing. Change the list any time with **Edit**. Each check uses your AI model, so a long list costs more than a short one.

**Three kinds.**
- **Free** briefings are put together by Talqava from what it already holds (your tasks, goals, files, or a list you typed). They use no AI model and cost nothing. Renewals, birthdays, the files tidy-up and the policy reminder write to you only when there is something to say.
- **Researched** briefings are done by an agent with your AI model, and each says about what it costs before you switch it on. They report; they change nothing.
- **Drafting** briefings (Overdue invoices, Inbox triage, Review replies, Quiet customers, Newsletter draft, Social posts, Meeting notes to actions, Weekly review and plan, New signup prospecting, After-hours holding replies, Proposal first draft) write drafts for you. They never send, post or change anything by themselves. They arrive under **Waiting on you**, for you to read, accept or send back.

**Answer a drafting briefing by number.** Its items are numbered 1, 2, 3. Write what to do with each in the box on its card, for example `1 send, 2 skip, 3 make it shorter`, and press **Send back with this comment**. The agent does exactly that for those numbers. Anything that sends or changes something still asks for your yes.

**Quiet unless there is something to say.** A card marked **Quiet** writes to you only when it matters: Website down alert (when your site stops loading, and again when it is back), Page change watch (when the page's words change, showing what was added and removed), Mentions watch, New video digest and Merge requests waiting (when there is something new), and the renewals, birthdays, tidy-up and policy reminders. So that silence never hides a fault, the two address watchers also write once a week to say they are still watching, and say so if the address cannot be read.

**Switch one on**
1. Press **Switch on** on its card. A card that says **Needs Xero** (or another service) shows **Connect … first** instead; connect the service, then come back.
2. Answer its one question if it has one. For the news you can leave it empty to follow your mission. For renewals and birthdays, type one on each line: a name, a comma, then the date (for example `Shop insurance, 2027-03-01`).
3. Read the short list: when it arrives, where, what it reads, what it costs, and that it changes nothing.
4. Under **Who sends it**, Talqava has chosen someone; choose another person on your team if this is their work. Under **AI model**, leave **The sender's usual model** or choose one for this briefing only. A briefing built from your own tasks uses no AI model, so it has no such choice.
5. Press **Switch on**.
6. The first one arrives straight away.

Talqava suggests the position whose job the briefing suits, for example your Bookkeeper for overdue invoices. It looks at departments first and then at job titles, so a Researcher is found even in a department called Strategy; when nobody fits, it suggests the first position on your chart. You can always choose someone else. If that agent could not search the web or use your connected services, switching the briefing on gives it that ability, and the list says so first.

**Edit one.** Press **Edit** on its card to change its answer (or its list of names and dates), when it arrives, who sends it and which AI model it uses. The change applies from the next one; press **Send one now** to see it straight away.

**Pause or switch one off.** On its card: **Pause**, **Send one now**, or **Switch off**. In the Inbox, an open briefing has **Pause** and **Edit** at its top.

**Your words decide.** What you type for a briefing is followed as written. For the Morning news, type exactly what you want covered, for example "What is going on around the world today" or "UK business news and football". It covers that and does not narrow it to your business. Leave it empty and it covers what matters to your business instead.

**A briefing just arrives.** It starts straight away each time: no plan to approve and no questions first, even when the position sending it is a lead.

**What to keep in mind.** Rules and deadlines and the Month-end pack are reminders and summaries, not legal, tax or accounting advice: confirm with your accountant. A briefing that reads a service can only report what that service holds.

## Key ideas

**An automation is a name, a time, someone to do it, and instructions.**

**Choosing when.** The **When** box gives you plain choices, all in your computer's time:

- **Every day**, **Every weekday (Monday to Friday)**, **Every week** (pick the day) or **Every month** (pick a date from the 1st to the 28th, which every month has), each at a time you choose.
- **Every few hours** or **Every few minutes**: pick how many.
- **Once**: pick a date and time. After it runs, the automation shows as paused.
- **Custom (advanced)**: for anything else, type the timing by hand, such as **every 30m**, **at 2027-01-15T09:00**, or a cron line such as **0 9 \* \* 1-5** (9:00 am on weekdays). The card still shows it in plain words when it can.

**Choosing who does it.**

- **The Talqava assistant, in a chat** (the standard choice). Each run starts a fresh chat titled with a clock (⏰) and the automation's name. It finishes with a short report.
- **An agent from your organization chart, as a task in their queue.** Each run becomes a task on the **Tasks** board for that agent, with the automation's name as its title. It waits its turn in the agent's queue like any other task. The agent can hand parts to the people below them, and the result comes back to you **To review**, where you accept it or send it back. This is the best choice for regular work that belongs to a department, such as a weekly sales pipeline for your Head of Sales.

**An automation belongs to the position.** When you put a different agent into a position (Organization → the position → change who fills it), its automations carry on with the new agent. A position's panel on the Organization page lists its automations, with **+ Automation** to add one.

If the position you chose leaves the chart, the assistant does the job instead, and the **Activity log** says so. The card tells you, and you can **Edit** it to choose someone else.

**Automations run while Talqava is open.** Talqava checks for due automations every few seconds. In the Mac app, closing the window keeps Talqava running in the background; choosing **Quit** stops it. If Talqava was stopped or your computer was off when an automation was due, it runs once as soon as you open Talqava again, then carries on as normal.

**Approvals still apply.** A run follows your **Approvals** choice in **Settings**:

- **Ask me before commands, file changes and API writes** (the standard). When a run wants to make a change, such as running a command, changing a file, sending data to another service or clicking in a web page, it stops and waits for you. It shows on **Agents** under **Waiting on you**.
- **Run everything without asking.** The run goes ahead on its own. Even so, once it has read outside content, such as web pages, every change asks you first.

Reading and searching never need approval. Your spending and step limits apply to routined runs too.

**You can also ask in a chat.** Say "every weekday at 8am, send me a summary of new orders", and the Talqava assistant (or any agent with the **Automations** power) sets it up. It then appears on this page, where you can change it.

## How to…

### Add an automation

1. Under **New automation**, type a **Name**, for example "Morning news digest".
2. In **When**, choose how often, then the day and time.
3. In **Who does it**, keep the assistant, or choose an agent from your organization.
4. In **Instructions**, write what should be done each time, as you would to a new colleague. The run can't ask you questions first, so include everything it needs: where to look, what to produce, and where to put it.
5. Press **Add automation**. The card shows when it runs next.

### Change an automation

1. Press **Edit** on its card.
2. Change the name, the time, who does it or the instructions.
3. Press **Save changes**. A new time starts counting from now, and a paused automation starts again.

### Try an automation straight away

1. Press **Run now** on its card.
2. If the assistant does it, the new chat opens so you can watch. If an agent does it, the **Tasks** board opens on the new task.

### See what an automation did

1. Press **Last run** on its card. It opens the latest run's chat, or its task on the **Tasks** board.
2. Read the report. For a task, accept it or send it back with a comment.

### Pause, resume or delete an automation

1. Press **Pause** to stop it for now. The card shows **paused**.
2. Press **Resume** to start it again; it runs at the next time due.
3. Press **Delete** and confirm to remove it. Past runs, their chats and tasks, are kept.

## Tips

- Press **Run now** after adding an automation, to check the instructions do what you expect.
- Give regular department work to that department's lead, so it lands in their queue and comes back to you for review.
- For an automation that makes changes, leave approvals on **Ask me…** until you trust it.
- Keep Talqava running, and your computer awake, at the times your automations are due.

## If something goes wrong

- **"Give the automation a name, e.g. Morning news digest."** Fill in **Name**.
- **"Write the instructions: what should be done each time?"** Fill in **Instructions**.
- **"That time is already in the past."** A **Once** date has already gone. Pick a future date and time.
- **"That time could not be read…"** A **Custom** automation was not in a form Talqava understands. Choose one of the plain options instead, or use one of the forms shown.
- **"The shortest interval is 1 minute."** Use **Every few minutes** with 1 or more.
- **"Choose an agent from your organization chart, or the Talqava assistant."** The agent you picked is not on the chart of the organization you have open. Choose again.
- **"That one-time job has already passed."** You pressed **Resume** on a **Once** automation that already ran. **Edit** it and pick a new date.
- **An automation seems stuck.** It is probably waiting for your approval (see **Waiting on you** on **Agents**), or, for an agent, waiting its turn in the queue (see **Tasks**).
